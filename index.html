The rules

The law

After the vote

What has to happen once a council's results are declared, how long it has, and what the law says when no party has a majority.

The timeline

  1. ResultsThe council is declared elected

    The Electoral Commission declares the result for each council. The 14-day deadlines run from the council's election.

  2. Within 14 daysThe first meeting is called

    The municipal manager must call the new council's first meeting within 14 days after it has been declared elected. If there is no municipal manager, the provincial MEC for local government designates someone.

    Structures Act s 29(2)
  3. First sittingA speaker is elected

    At its first sitting, the council must elect a speaker from among its councillors. The municipal manager presides over that election.

    Structures Act s 36(2)–(3)
  4. Within 14 daysA mayor is elected

    A council that has an executive mayor must elect one at a meeting held within 14 days after the council's election. A council with an executive committee must name its members within the same period, and elects the mayor from that committee.

    Structures Act ss 45, 48, 55
  5. OptionalA whip

    The council may also elect a whip at its first sitting. The speaker presides over that vote.

    Structures Act s 41A
  6. Within a prescribed periodA development plan

    The new council must adopt an integrated development plan (IDP), or adopt its predecessor's, within a prescribed period after the start of its term. Until then, the previous council's plan stays in force.

    Systems Act s 25

How the votes work

  • Candidates are nominated on a form signed by two councillors, and the nominee must accept in writing. There is no debate.
  • If only one candidate is nominated, that person is declared elected.
  • If there is more than one, councillors present vote by secret ballot, one vote each. The winner needs a majority of the votes.
  • If nobody gets a majority, the candidate with the fewest votes is eliminated and councillors vote again, until someone has a majority.
  • If the last two candidates tie, a further meeting must be held within seven days. If they tie again there, the winner is decided by lot.
  • More than half of the council's councillors must be present when any vote is taken.
Structures Act Schedule 3 · s 30(1)

Hung councils

When no party has more than half the seats

No party can elect a speaker or mayor on its own votes, so parties have to agree whom to back. The winner of an election for speaker or mayor needs a majority of the votes cast by the councillors present, not necessarily more than half of all seats.

Structures Act Schedule 3, item 6

Some decisions, including passing the budget, need the support of a majority of all councillors, not just of those voting.

Structures Act s 30(2) · Constitution s 160(2) and (3)(b)

The Act has no rules on coalitions

The Structures Act does not regulate coalition agreements, and it does not say what happens if a council misses the 14-day deadline.

The Municipal Structures Amendment Bill, introduced in the National Assembly by the minister of cooperative governance on 18 August 2026, would extend the deadline to 30 days and require coalition agreements to be published in the Provincial Gazette. It would also allow a notice of a motion to remove the speaker, whip, executive mayor or members of an executive committee only once every 12 months, except in cases such as misconduct or breaking the law.

It is not law. The bill is still at committee stage: Parliament's cooperative governance committee was first briefed on it on 27 August and is taking public comment until 27 November 2026. It must then be passed by the National Assembly and the National Council of Provinces and signed by the President. It was not finalised before the elections, so councils elected on 4 November must meet the current 14-day deadlines.

Municipal Structures Amendment Bill [B23—2026] · PMG bill tracker, 4 November 2026; bill text and Parliament statement of 28 August, checked 9 November 2026

Dissolving a council

A council can dissolve itself only with a two-thirds vote at a meeting called for that purpose, and only once two years have passed since it was elected.

Structures Act s 34(1)–(2)

The provincial MEC for local government may dissolve a council in line with section 139 of the Constitution, which governs provincial intervention in municipalities.

Structures Act s 34(4)

After a dissolution

If the MEC dissolves a council, the MEC must appoint one or more administrators to keep the municipality running until a new council is elected.

Structures Act s 35

A by-election must then be held within 90 days of the dissolution, called by the MEC after consulting the Electoral Commission. The exception: no by-election is called if the next general municipal election is due within nine months, or if the MEC and the minister decide it should wait for that election.

Structures Act s 25(1)(c), (3), (6)
